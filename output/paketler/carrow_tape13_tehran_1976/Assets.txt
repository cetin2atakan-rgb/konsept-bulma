## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

The night of 18 to 19 September 1976, over a capital city in the Middle East. After an hour of calls from the ground about a brilliant light cycling through blue, green, red and orange, two interceptors are sent up one after the other. As each closes in, its radio and instruments fail, and come back once it turns away. The second pilot gets a radar lock, watches a smaller light break away and descend toward the desert, and loses his weapons systems as he tries to follow. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977, less than a year after it happened.

Wednesday, the 31st of August, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

The newest file in Raymond Keel's drawer arrives through ordinary intelligence channels with a memo calling it "a classic." For the first time, Keel lays out the pattern he has been circling for three tapes: a compass in 1947, a sonar contact in 1967, two cockpits in 1976. He says who he thinks has been watching for it. He does not yet say what.
