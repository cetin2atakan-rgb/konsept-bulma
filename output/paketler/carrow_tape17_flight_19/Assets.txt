## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

5 December 1945, a routine training flight off the Florida coast. Five torpedo bombers carrying fourteen men leave on a navigation exercise. The flight leader reports that both his compasses are off and that he thinks he is over the Keys. Another pilot keeps saying his compass points them home, and is overruled. That night a flying boat with thirteen men aboard goes out to search for them. A tanker sees a fireball on the water. None of the six aircraft, and none of the 27 men, is ever found. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Saturday, the 10th of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

Raymond Keel reads the Navy's own transcript and sets aside most of the Triangle legend that grew up around it. He points to the rescue plane's documented fuel-vapor hazard and a weather report that contradicts the paperbacks. That leaves one brief radar return the file would not throw away, and a mother who spent years getting the words "pilot error" taken out of her son's record.
