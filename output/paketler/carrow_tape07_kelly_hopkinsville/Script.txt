File B-029. Class B, persons, the same letter I gave you for the West Virginia hillside, though this file is three years younger than that one and, I think, considerably better documented. Event: the night of August twenty-first, nineteen fifty-five, a farmhouse outside a small town in western Kentucky. Disposition: blank.

Two families, eleven people in total, men, women, and children, were gathered at a modest farmhouse that evening when the first of them, stepping outside toward a well near dusk, reported a bright light descending into a gully a short distance behind the house. Within a few hours, by every one of the eleven accounts taken separately over the following days, something came up out of that gully toward the house itself, and by the time the night was finished, this family had fired, by a conservative count taken from spent casings and later corroborated by the investigating officers, more than fifty rounds from more than one weapon at something standing, at various points, outside their windows, on their roof, and in the branches of a tree at the edge of their yard.

I want to say, before I go any further, that I have given a version of this introduction eleven times tonight in my head before committing it to tape, because I think this file sits at an unusual angle to the other six. [BLOK4_DUVARDAKI_KARELER]
The first item I want to describe is a sketch, composited from eleven independent descriptions taken over the following three days, showing a small humanoid figure, roughly three feet in height, with an oversized head, enormous ears set high and wide on either side of it, and large eyes that every witness independently described as glowing with a pale yellowish light of their own, not merely reflecting a lantern or a flashlight the way an animal's eyes might. The limbs are long relative to the body, ending in hands the witnesses describe, almost uniformly, as clawed, and the skin, in every description, is given as silvery or metallic rather than fleshy, catching available light the way the lunar prints I showed you on the first tape caught theirs.

I want to note, too, that none of the eleven witnesses, questioned separately over the following three days, described the ears as pointed in the way popular illustrations of this case would later render them. Several specifically use the word "wide" rather than "pointed," more like a bat's ear held rigid than anything resembling the delicate, tapered shape that later decades of retelling eventually settled on. I mention this because I think it is a useful reminder, after the bloodstained sword that turned out not to be bloodstained and the invented breakfast from two tapes ago, that popular memory tends to sand a strange true story down into a shape that is easier to draw, and I would rather give you the family's own clumsier, more specific words than the smoother version that image has since become in the popular imagination.

The second item is the sequence of events as the family itself reconstructed it for investigators, and I want to give it to you close to the order it was given to me, because the order matters. After the light was seen descending, the family went about an ordinary evening for perhaps two hours, with no further disturbance, until the family dog began barking with unusual intensity at something near the edge of the yard. One of the men stepped outside to investigate and reported seeing, briefly, a glowing shape moving low near the ground, dismissed at the time, by his own account, as possibly an animal catching the moonlight strangely. It was perhaps twenty minutes later that the same man, looking out a side window, saw a small figure matching the composite sketch approaching the house directly, hands raised, by his account, in a manner he could not help but read, at the time, as a gesture rather than an accident of movement.

The third item is the detail that every single account across eleven witnesses agrees on without exception, and the detail I find hardest to set beside anything else in this drawer. The family opened fire on the figure at close range, through a window and then, as the night progressed, from the open doorway and the yard itself, repeatedly, with a shotgun and a rifle both, at a range investigators later measured as, in several instances, under twenty feet. The figure, struck directly by the family's own unanimous account, did not fall the way a struck man or animal falls. It flipped backward, in more than one witness's specific word, "like a tumbling toy," and then righted itself and withdrew, unhurried, into the dark, only to be seen again, by a different family member, within the hour, at a different window or on the roof itself, in what the family came to describe, over the course of that night, as either several creatures taking turns or one creature returning again and again undeterred by what should, by any ordinary accounting, have been a mortal wound.

The fourth item is a physical sample this office's predecessor was able to obtain during the initial investigation, a scraping taken from a porch post where, according to the family, one shot had struck the creature from extremely close range as it reached toward a window. The sample card describes a faint, thin streak of residue on the wood, greenish in tint, that laboratory analysis at the time was unable to match to any paint, lubricant, or biological substance in its reference collection, noting only that it was "not consistent with blood of any species examined" and leaving the matter there, unresolved, the way so much of this drawer leaves things.

I want to add, because I think fairness requires it, that a later and more skeptical reanalysis, conducted years after the original sample was taken, suggested the residue might plausibly have come from an ordinary wood preservative or an old coat of paint reacting unpredictably with weather and time, a mundane explanation I want to give its full due rather than dismiss simply because it is less interesting than the alternative. I find it less persuasive than I might otherwise, only because the family's own account places the mark at the exact point of contact they described in real time, before any investigator had told them where to look, which is a harder coincidence for an ordinary paint reaction to explain away than it would be if the mark had simply been found somewhere convenient after the fact.

The fifth item is one I have not seen discussed in any outside account of this case, and I want to be careful describing it because I only found it myself on a third pass through this folder two nights ago. A single pane of glass from one of the farmhouse's windows, removed and retained by the original investigators for reasons the file does not fully explain, shows, under close examination, a faint arc of evenly spaced marks along its lower edge, as though something had dragged a row of fine, blunt points across the surface without quite breaking it. I measured the spacing myself against a photograph of the band from the object I described to you on the last tape, and against the patrolman's sketch from two tapes before that, and I am not going to tell you they match, because I do not think they do, not precisely. I am going to tell you that the spacing is close enough, across all three, that I no longer believe I am looking at three separate coincidences.

I want to describe one more exchange from the witness statements before I move on, because it is the detail that has stayed with me longer than almost anything else in this folder. One of the younger family members, a boy no older than twelve that night, described, in his own separate statement taken apart from the adults, a moment when one of the creatures came close enough to a window that he could see, in his own words, "its mouth wasn't a mouth like ours, more like a line that didn't open, and it never made a sound the whole night, not once, not even when we hit it." Eleven people, firing more than fifty rounds over several hours at a creature returning again and again, and not one of them, in any statement, describes it making a single sound in response — no cry, no snarl, nothing that any struck animal, however strange, would ordinarily produce. I have sat with that silence longer than I have sat with almost any other single detail in this drawer.

There is a sixth item I want to mention, a small one, easy to miss in the file's own index. The family's dog, who had barked with such unusual intensity at the start of the evening, is noted in a follow-up visit conducted some weeks later as having refused, from that night onward, to go anywhere near the tree line behind the house, a change in behavior the family itself found strange enough to mention unprompted to the investigator who returned to ask a handful of follow-up questions.

[BLOK5_ONLAR_HAKKINDA]
I want to say something about the family and the investigators both, because this file treats each of them more fairly than most of the files in this drawer treat anyone. The investigating officers who responded that night — summoned when the family finally fled to town, shaken and, by the responding officer's own written account, "clearly and genuinely terrified, not intoxicated, not hysterical in any theatrical sense" — searched the property thoroughly, found the bullet holes the family described exactly where the family described them, in window screens, in porch posts, in the siding itself, and found no blood, no bodies, and no evidence, in the responding officer's own words, "of any hoax a family of this size and these means could plausibly have staged in the available time." The file does not conclude that something otherworldly visited that farmhouse. [BLOK6_KISA_SAPMA_OWLS]
A short digression, because this file has its own version of the owl explanation I gave you from an earlier tape, and I think it deserves the same fair hearing.

A popular explanation that followed this case for years afterward proposed that the family had mistaken a type of large owl, startled and reacting defensively, for something stranger, aided by darkness, fear, and the understandable unfamiliarity most people of that era had with how such a bird actually looks and behaves up close. I want to give this theory its full and fair consideration, the way I gave the owl theory its consideration on an earlier tape, because the comparison genuinely is not unreasonable on its face — certain owls, when harassed or defending territory, will dive at a perceived threat repeatedly, and a frightened family firing blindly in the dark at a repeatedly returning shape is not, on the surface, an implausible scenario.

What this explanation does not account for, in my reading, is the consistency of eleven separate accounts regarding height, proportion, and especially the hands — no owl has hands, clawed or otherwise, and no witness among the eleven describes anything resembling wings, feathers, or a beak, details that would be difficult for even a frightened rural family, entirely unfamiliar with owls up close, to misplace so completely and so uniformly across that many separate tellings. I also do not think the owl explanation accounts for the greenish residue on the porch post, which no bird, however startled, leaves behind. I think, as with the barn owl theory before it, this is a plausible-sounding explanation reached for because it was available and reassuring, not because it actually explains the specific physical evidence this file preserves.

I want to add one further comparison, because I think it strengthens rather than weakens the family's own account. Investigators at the time noted, and I have confirmed independently in a separate wildlife reference this office keeps on hand, that the owl species native to that part of Kentucky stand under two feet tall even at their largest, with a wingspan that, however startling in darkness, does not produce a silhouette anyone familiar with the bird would reasonably describe as three feet of upright, bipedal, clawed figure standing at a window at eye level with an adult man. The theory requires not one misidentification but a whole chain of them, compounding across eleven separate witnesses, each one somehow arriving independently at the same specific, consistent, and distinctly non-avian description.

I want to note, too, a secondary theory that circulated alongside the owl explanation, proposing that a visiting circus or carnival might have lost a trained monkey or similar small animal in the area around that time, startling the family into mistaking an escaped performer's animal for something stranger in the dark. No circus or carnival reporting a missing animal of any kind was ever identified anywhere in the region during that period, despite what the file notes was a genuine effort to track one down, and no monkey of any known species produces hands the eleven witnesses would plausibly describe as clawed, or skin they would independently describe as metallic rather than furred. I include the theory mainly to show you how far an investigator of that era was willing to reach for an ordinary explanation before concluding, as this file ultimately does, that no ordinary explanation was available to reach for.

[BLOK7_NE_OLDUKLARI]
One opinion per file, and I want to give you mine plainly, because I think this file, more than almost any other in this drawer, rewards plainness over theory.

I think eleven people saw exactly what they said they saw, and that what they saw was not an owl, not a hoax, and not a shared hallucination brought on by fear alone, because fear alone does not leave bullet holes in a specific, physically consistent pattern across multiple structures, and it does not leave a greenish residue a period laboratory could not identify. I think something was present at that farmhouse that night that behaved, under gunfire, in a manner inconsistent with any living creature native to that part of the world, and I think the family's own restraint — fleeing to town rather than continuing to escalate, and giving a consistent, modest account afterward rather than embellishing it for attention — argues strongly for their basic honesty rather than against it.

I want to add one final thought to this opinion, because I think it connects this file to everything else I have shown you across this drawer in a way I have not said out loud until tonight. Six files, six decades spread unevenly across more than a century, six different kinds of evidence — a photograph, a material, a sketch, a measurement, a letter, a convoy — and in every single one, ordinary, unremarkable people encountered something that did not behave the way the physical world around them was supposed to behave, and were then told, by someone with more authority than evidence, that they were mistaken. I no longer think that is coincidence, pattern, or probability. I think it is a policy, applied consistently, across a span of time longer than any one government department's institutional memory should reasonably be able to sustain without a great deal of deliberate effort.
