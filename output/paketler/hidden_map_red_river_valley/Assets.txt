## DESCRIPTION
The Red River Valley along the North Dakota–Minnesota border was never carved by its river. It is the exposed bed of Glacial Lake Agassiz, a meltwater lake larger than any lake on the continent today, and the river only began running across it about 9,300 years ago. Retired geography teacher Walter Hale counts down 25 geographic facts about the valley, from Wahpeton and Fargo-Moorhead up to Grand Forks: a river that runs north toward Hudson Bay, old beach ridges that roads still follow, the black clay that grows about a third of America's sugar beets, the 1888 Children's Blizzard, the Fargo tornado that helped create the Fujita Scale, the 1997 flood that crested five feet above the forecast, and a Viking ship built in a potato warehouse.
