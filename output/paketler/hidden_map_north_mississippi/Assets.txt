## DESCRIPTION
East of Sardis, a nearly three-mile earthen wall that the Army Corps of Engineers once called the largest earthen dam in the world holds back the Little Tallahatchie. It is one of four flood control lakes built across North Mississippi's hill country after the 1927 flood. Retired geography teacher Walter Hale counts down 25 geographic facts about the hills between Holly Springs, Oxford and Tupelo: yellow loess bluffs blown in by Ice Age winds, the cavalry raid that burned Grant's supply depot, the creek-bank treaty that drew today's county lines, the river that gave Faulkner the name Yoknapatawpha, a county with two courthouses, Elvis's two-room birthplace, and the hill country blues of Como and Chulahoma.
