## DESCRIPTION
Under the grass of central Nebraska lies the largest dune field in the entire Western Hemisphere: roughly 19,000 to 23,000 square miles of sand, held in place by roots only a few feet deep and sitting on top of one of the thickest parts of the Ogallala Aquifer. Retired geography teacher Walter Hale counts down 25 geographic facts about the Nebraska Sandhills: Carhenge, a 54-foot waterfall on private land, the first International Dark Sky Park in Nebraska, a national forest planted entirely by hand, nearly two thousand lakes tucked between the dunes, and a county with less than one person per square mile.
