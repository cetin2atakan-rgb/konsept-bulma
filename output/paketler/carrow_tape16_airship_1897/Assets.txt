## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

From the end of 1896 into the spring of 1897, years before anyone flew a powered airplane, newspapers across the country report a cigar-shaped craft lit by lamps, moving against the wind. In April a small Texas town reports that one struck a windmill and exploded, and that its pilot was buried in the local cemetery. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Thursday, the 8th of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

Raymond Keel opens the lowest-numbered file in the entire cabinet and sets the famous windmill crash aside as almost certainly invented. What he can't set aside: notarized affidavits signed by judges, physicians and a minister in towns too far apart to have compared notes, a scrap of alloy two laboratories couldn't agree on, and a recent cemetery investigation that stopped after a gravestone was vandalized. In the margin is the same pencil hand again: "Carrow's interest noted, local matter resolved quietly."
