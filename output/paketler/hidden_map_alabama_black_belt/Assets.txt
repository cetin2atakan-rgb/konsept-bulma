## DESCRIPTION
A few feet under the dark soil of west-central Alabama lies Cretaceous chalk, the floor of a sea that covered this land some seventy million years ago. That chalk made the soil, the soil made the cotton economy, and that economy shaped everything that followed here, all the way to the Edmund Pettus Bridge. Retired geography teacher Walter Hale counts down 25 geographic facts about Alabama's Black Belt, around Selma, Demopolis, Marion, Greensboro and Uniontown: the White Cliffs of Epes, shark teeth turned up in plowed fields, Old Cahawba (Alabama's vanished first capital), a vice president sworn in in Cuba who died weeks later near that town, the Gee's Bend quilters, and the voting rights campaign that began on a February night in Marion.
