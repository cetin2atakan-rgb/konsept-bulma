## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

November 1966, a river town in West Virginia. Two young couples driving past an abandoned wartime munitions site report a tall gray figure with wing-like limbs and eyes that throw back red light. For thirteen months the reports keep coming. Then on 15 December 1967 the highway bridge over the river collapses during the evening rush and 46 people die. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Thursday, the 15th of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

Raymond Keel pulls the two halves of this file apart. One is a run of sightings he partly explains with a large migrating bird and old chemical contamination, and partly can't. The other is a bridge brought down by a fatigued eyebar and a monitoring recommendation nobody followed up. He also answers the best-selling book that tied the two together, written by a man who happens to share his surname. Then, in the margin of that ignored inspection report, he finds the pencil hand again: "Carrow's office requested a copy, cause noted, no further action authorized."
