## BEAT 1 — What preload actually is — 0:38–1:28

> Start with the twelfth. Preload opens.
>
> PlayStation's own listing calls it a seven day preload. The game downloads to your console a week early.
>
> It does not let you play early. The files sit there locked until your region's unlock time. Preloading buys you nothing except not downloading on launch day.
>
> And that matters more than usual this year, because nobody will tell us how big the file is. The best number out there is two hundred and ten gigabytes, and that came from a leak account, not Rockstar.
>
> Rockstar also has not said what time on the twelfth preload opens. Just the date.
>
> One more thing about the twelfth. That is also the day the physical boxes hit shelves. And there is no disc in the box. It is a download code. The box exists so you can type the code in and start preloading.
>
> So if you bought the physical version to lend it to your brother, or trade it in — you cannot.

## BEAT 2 — Where two hundred gigabytes has to live — 1:28–2:48

> One more thing about the twelfth. This is the part that actually ruins people's night.
>
> Preloading means the file has to go somewhere, and on both consoles where it can go is restricted.
>
> On PS five, a game can sit on an external USB drive. It cannot run from one. GTA six has to be on your internal SSD, or on an M dot two drive you installed inside the console. So the usual trick, shoving things onto a USB drive to free up room, does not work for this one.
>
> Xbox is the same idea. Internal storage, or the official expansion card. Nothing else.
>
> And you need more free space than the file is big. VGTimes put the base install at a hundred and fifty to two hundred gigabytes, and said keep two hundred to two hundred and fifty free, because of the day one patch.
>
> So here is the thing to do today, not on the twelfth. Go and look at how much space you actually have.
>
> Because the worst version of the twelfth is finding out at midnight that you have to uninstall four games before you can even start.
>
> And do not wait for Rockstar to tell you the number first. Fans expected an official file size in late October, off insider claims about review code going out. Game Rant called that pure speculation.
>
> If there is still no official size by the twelfth, you are clearing space blind. So size it off the leak and leave yourself room. Not off a Rockstar figure that might never arrive.

## BEAT 4 — Is this actually sinister? — 3:39–4:39

> Let me be fair about something, because I have seen a lot of videos this week that are not.
>
> A review embargo lifting the day before launch is normal. That is not Rockstar being shady. Most big games do exactly that.
>
> And review copies going out roughly a month early, for a game this size, is also normal.
>
> So the embargo on its own is not the story.
>
> The story is the combination. A seven day preload, starting six days before anyone is allowed to tell you if the game is good.
>
> That part is unusual. Normally preload and reviews land close together. Here there is a six day gap, and in that gap, two hundred and ten gigabytes goes onto your console and the money is already gone.
>
> That is not a conspiracy. It is just a schedule that happens to work out very well for Rockstar and not for you.
>
> The community already has a name for it. They call it the preload catch. Money first, information second.
>
> I think that name is fair. I just do not think it needs the outrage, because once you know the fourth date it stops being a trap at all.
