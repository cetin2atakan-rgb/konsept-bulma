## DESCRIPTION
Off the shore near Port Austin, Turnip Rock rises out of Lake Huron, a mushroom-shaped stack of stone that waves have carved wider at the top than at the bottom. Every acre of land around it is private, so the only way to see it is to paddle there. Retired geography teacher Walter Hale counts down 25 geographic facts about Michigan's Thumb, from Bad Axe and Caseville to Sebewaing and Harbor Beach: the glacier that split around hard sandstone and gave the mitten its thumb, the 1881 fire that burned about a million acres in a single day and became the American Red Cross's first disaster relief, a lighthouse standing alone two and a half miles out in the lake, Michigan's only known prehistoric rock carvings, a quarry town that made most of America's grindstones, and the buried drainage tile that turned swamp into bean and sugar beet country.
