## Description
In the United States, flipping a light switch up turns it on. In the United Kingdom, down is on. Why do two countries with the same language and the same early electrical engineering disagree on something this simple?
The answer has almost nothing to do with electricity and almost everything to do with a dangerous piece of 1880s factory hardware: the open knife switch.

This video follows the switch from Edison's Pearl Street Station in 1882 to the smart switches of today: the gravity rule for knife switches that shaped American practice, John Henry Holmes and the quick-break mechanism, the Newton and Goldberg toggle switch patent, Underwriters Laboratories and the National Electrical Code, the British fireman's switch, and why Britain settled into "down is on" through trade habit rather than one national rule. Along the way: Japan's split 50 Hz and 60 Hz grid, plug shapes, and why an early choice can become too expensive to ever undo.
