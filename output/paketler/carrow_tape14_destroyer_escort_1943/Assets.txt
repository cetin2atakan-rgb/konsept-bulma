## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

September 1943, a newly commissioned destroyer escort on her shakedown cruise. The quartermaster's log has her moored at a navy yard for four days. The engineering officer's log for the same four days has her at sea running degaussing trials. A crewman's letter home describes warm, clear water like the Caribbean. There is also a requisition for large coil assemblies, a dry-dock photograph with two faces out of focus, and a medical note for crewmen treated for disorientation and memory loss, with no names. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Saturday, the 3rd of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

This is the ship most people know only from the legend of invisibility and sailors fused into steel. Raymond Keel sets that legend aside. He doesn't believe it. Then he reads what the paperwork actually says, closes out this run of tapes, and tells us that someone upstairs has asked him for a written summary, "in the cataloguer's own words, unedited."
