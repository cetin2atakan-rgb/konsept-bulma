## DESCRIPTION
Less than four percent of North America's tallgrass prairie survives, and most of what is left sits in the Flint Hills of Kansas. The reason is in the ground: a band of chert-studded Permian limestone just under the topsoil that broke the plows of every settler who tried to farm it. Retired geography teacher Walter Hale counts down 25 geographic facts about the Flint Hills, from the two million acres ranchers burn on purpose every spring (you can see the smoke from orbit) and the bison herd returned to the Tallgrass Prairie National Preserve, to the oak grove that named Council Grove and the oil field found by geology instead of luck.
