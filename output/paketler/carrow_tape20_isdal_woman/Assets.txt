## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

29 November 1970, a remote valley outside a coastal city in Norway. Hikers find a woman's badly burned body. Every label and maker's mark has been cut out of her clothes before the fire. At the railway station her luggage holds cash in several currencies, wigs, and passports under different names, all with her photograph. In the months before, she had stayed at hotels in several countries under different identities. Her notebook is written in a private code. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Tuesday, the 20th of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

Raymond Keel opens a file on a woman his office still cannot name. It arrived through the same "reciprocal exchange arrangement" as the Soviet file. He reads forgeries too good for an amateur, banknotes traced to a government reserve, detectives who felt the case being slowed down around them, and a grave marked only with a number where flowers still appear. In the margin of the witness statement about the men she met is the familiar pencil hand: "Carrow's office requested this statement withheld from further circulation."
