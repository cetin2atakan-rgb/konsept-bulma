## Description
Is crossing your legs bad for you after 65? Mostly no, but it can push a blood pressure reading up by several points, and long stretches of sitting still are the real risk.
Educational video presented by Doctor Marcus Reid, a fictional educational persona. This is not medical advice.

We rank five versions of this habit, from harmless to the combination worth your attention: occasional crossing, crossing during a blood pressure check, long unbroken stretches of sitting, crossing when you already have venous insufficiency, and crossed legs combined with a day of almost no movement. Along the way: the popliteal vein, the calf muscle pump, venous valves, and why the evidence on crossed legs and varicose veins is weaker than most videos suggest.

Two adjustments that take under a minute: the uncrossed check before any blood pressure reading (feet flat, back supported, five minutes of quiet sitting), and a short movement break every 20 to 30 minutes.

## PINNED COMMENT
Quick clarification: the Edinburgh Vein Study did not find a consistent link between sitting habits and developing varicose veins, so this video does not claim crossing your legs causes them. What is well documented is the blood pressure reading effect and the risk from long periods of sitting still. If you have diagnosed venous insufficiency or notice swelling in one leg only, talk to your doctor directly.
