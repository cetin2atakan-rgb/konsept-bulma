## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

Shag Harbour, Nova Scotia, the evening of 4 October 1967. Witnesses along the shore watch an object come down at a shallow angle, its lights going round in order, and strike the water with a sound like a whistle cut short. A pale light floats offshore while the town gathers to watch, then goes under. Boats, the coast guard and navy divers search for days and find nothing at all. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Sunday, the 28th of August, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

For once, Raymond Keel is reading a file its own government openly left unresolved: no wreckage, no fuel, no missing aircraft, no cover story. Only a foam patch that would not break up, a single sonar contact that was never found again, and, in the margin of the naval search report, the same pencil hand he has now seen four times, pointing to a standing order that was already in place before the night it happened.
