## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

1946, less than a year after the war. From spring to autumn, thousands of people across Scandinavia report slender, finned, rocket-shaped objects crossing the sky, and several are seen falling into lakes. Military divers drag those lakes and find nothing. A ground radar log records one object on a steady ballistic course that suddenly makes a sharp turn, which no rocket of the time could do. A senior Allied air officer flies in to look at the evidence and concludes that it matches no known weapon. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Friday, the 23rd of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

Raymond Keel closes this run of tapes with the file that explains so many of the others. In its cover correspondence is a 1946 memorandum setting up "an informal technical exchange on aerial phenomena of mutual concern" between allied intelligence services. Keel thinks that arrangement is how the Soviet and Norwegian files reached his drawer, and that Carrow may have begun as nothing stranger than a committee that never disbanded. At the end of the tape he reads the first reply from upstairs.
