## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

December 1900, a lighthouse on a remote rock off the coast of Scotland. The relief boat, held back nearly two weeks by storms, lands after Christmas and finds the gate shut, the door shut, and nobody on the island. Inside: a stopped clock, unmade beds, an overturned chair, and one oilskin coat still on its peg, so one of the three keepers went out into the weather without it. At the western landing the iron railings are twisted far above the usual reach of the sea, yet a ship that passed that same week logged calm water. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Tuesday, the 6th of September, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

Raymond Keel has sent his summary upstairs and heard nothing back. While he waits, he opens the quietest file in his drawer: no creature, no light, no failing instrument. There is only a crossed-out line in the relief keeper's telegram draft, a note on the cover sheet in a hand he has never seen before, and the outline of a second folder that once sat beside this one and has since been taken away.
