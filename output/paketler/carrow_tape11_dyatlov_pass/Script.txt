[BLOK4_DUVARDAKI_KARELER]
The first item is a photograph of the group's tent as it was found, more than three weeks after the fact, partially collapsed under snow on an exposed slope, with a long cut in its fabric wall, made, according to the forensic notes attached, from the inside rather than the outside, and made, crucially, with deliberate, controlled strokes rather than the ragged tearing a panicked hand makes in a hurry. I want to sit on that detail, because I think it is the single hardest piece of evidence in this entire file to reconcile with any ordinary explanation. Nine people, in the dark, in temperatures well below freezing, cut their own shelter open from within, carefully enough to leave clean lines rather than frantic ones, and then left it, most of them in socks or partially dressed, without the outer clothing and boots that were later found still inside.

I want to describe the cut itself one more time, in slightly more technical terms than I used a moment ago, because I think the precision matters to anyone trying to picture it honestly. The forensic note describes a single, continuous incision roughly knee to shoulder height, made from a position inside the tent facing outward, consistent with one person, working carefully, in the dark, likely by feel rather than sight, taking the time to produce a clean opening rather than simply slashing at the fabric indiscriminately. I have tried, more than once this week, to picture the particular state of mind that produces careful, deliberate work with a blade while simultaneously believing your life depends on getting outside as quickly as possible, and I have not been able to reconcile those two things into a single coherent human reaction, which is, I think, the entire reason this file has never closed properly in any country that has ever held it.

The second item is a set of photographs showing a trail of footprints leading away from the tent down the slope, eight or nine sets depending on which analysis of the snow this file includes, walking rather than running at first, according to the spacing, for a considerable distance before the trail becomes harder to follow. I want to be plain about what this means: these were not people fleeing in blind panic from the first step. Something made them cut their way out and walk, deliberately, away from the only shelter they had, into conditions that would kill most of them within hours.

I want to note the formation of the footprints as well, because the file's own analysis spends considerable attention on it. The group did not scatter in different directions the way a crowd fleeing in pure panic typically disperses. They moved, for the visible length of the trail, roughly together, in a loose but discernible group, down a specific bearing that happened to lead away from the tent and toward the tree line below, as though whatever they were responding to had a direction, or at least a side of the tent they were all independently moving away from rather than a danger surrounding them on every side at once.

The third item describes the first two bodies recovered, found at the edge of a wooded area near the base of a large cedar tree, partially undressed, with evidence of a small fire having been built and allowed to burn out, and with injuries to their hands and the tree's own lower branches consistent, according to the forensic note, with one or both having climbed it, apparently looking at something or trying to see something from height, before climbing back down.

The fourth item describes three more bodies, found at increasing distances back toward the tent, in postures the investigating team recorded as suggesting they had been attempting to return to it, one of them found with injuries to the head that the forensic report attributes to a fall, though the particular severity of that fall is never fully reconciled with the terrain where the body was found.

The fifth item, and the one I find hardest to read aloud even now, describes the final four bodies, not recovered until the snow began melting months later, found in a ravine some distance from the others, under a considerable depth of snow, three of them with injuries the forensic report describes in a clinical language I am going to paraphrase rather than quote directly: severe internal trauma to the chest and skull, comparable, in the report's own stated opinion, to the kind of force produced by a significant fall or an impact none of the terrain at that location could plausibly explain, without a single corresponding external wound, bruise, or broken skin to account for how that force was delivered. One of the four is additionally noted as missing both eyes and a portion of the tongue, a detail the original investigation attributes, without much elaboration, to the effects of exposure, decomposition, and scavenging animals over the following months, an explanation I want to give its full and fair weight precisely because I think it is the single detail in this file most likely to be sensationalized beyond what the evidence actually supports.

I want to be careful and specific about why I am giving that particular explanation so much weight, because I think restraint here matters more than almost anywhere else in this folder. Small scavengers and the slow work of months of exposure in a ravine are entirely capable of producing exactly this kind of damage to soft tissue, and a body recovered that long after death, in those conditions, is not comparable to a body examined within hours. Whoever read this file before me underlined this detail twice in pencil, as though it were the strangest thing in the entire case, and I do not think the evidence supports that particular emphasis. I think the cut tent and the controlled, hours-long sequence of actions that followed it are considerably stranger, and considerably better supported, than anything to do with the missing eyes and tongue, and I want to say so plainly rather than let the more lurid detail crowd out the one that actually deserves the attention.

The sixth item is a radiological sample card, noting that clothing recovered from several of the final four bodies tested positive for elevated levels of radioactive contamination, at a level the card describes as "above background, within range associated with contaminated industrial clothing," a finding the original investigation apparently did not pursue to any stated conclusion, closing the broader case not long afterward.

I want to note that radiological contamination of this kind was not, in that era and that part of the world, an entirely unheard-of finding on ordinary clothing, given the region's proximity to a number of industrial and military facilities with known histories of contamination incidents during that decade. I raise this because I think it is the responsible, ordinary explanation available for this particular detail, and I do not want to present it as more mysterious than it may actually be. What I have not been able to find, anywhere in this file, is a specific facility, incident, or exposure pathway identified by the original investigators as the source, only the plain fact of the readings themselves and the absence of any further inquiry into where they came from, which leaves the mundane explanation available to me without actually being confirmed by anything in the file itself.

There is a seventh item I want to describe, a short note appended by the original investigator rather than by this office's own analysts, recording that several of the recovered personal effects, including a torch found lying on the roof of the tent, were sent for separate laboratory analysis and never returned to the case file, with no further record of that analysis anywhere in the materials I have been given. I have come to recognize this particular pattern now across eleven tapes, regardless of which country's filing habits I happen to be reading — an item sent away for analysis, and a silence afterward that the file itself never bothers to explain or apologize for.

I also want to mention an eighth item, a page of weather data for the region covering the days surrounding the event, confirming unusually severe wind conditions that night, strong enough on their own to have made survival difficult for anyone caught outside without proper clothing. I give this detail its full weight, because I think it explains plainly why nearly everyone who left that tent died of exposure regardless of whatever drove them to leave it in the first place. It does not, on its own, explain why they left.

[BLOK5_ONLAR_HAKKINDA]
I want to say something about the investigator who closed this file, because I think his final written conclusion, translated and preserved in this folder, is one of the most quietly remarkable sentences in this entire drawer. Under what this file's own cover note describes as considerable pressure from above to attribute the deaths to a mundane cause and close the matter cleanly, he instead recorded, as the official cause, a phrase that translates roughly as "a compelling natural force," one the hikers were unable to overcome, naming no avalanche, no storm, and no person, and refusing, as far as this folder's translation reflects, to force a tidier conclusion onto evidence he plainly did not believe supported one. The case file was then sealed by his own government, and as far as anyone has been able to tell me, it remains sealed today, eighteen years later, which I think tells you at least as much about what his superiors made of that honesty as any sentence he was permitted to write himself.

I want to add a detail about the search and recovery effort itself, because the sheer scale of it, undertaken in brutal winter conditions by a government not generally known, in that era, for extending that kind of sustained effort toward nine ordinary private citizens, has stayed with me since I first read the file's logistics summary. Military aircraft, extensive ground teams, and a search lasting the better part of three months before the final four bodies were recovered — a commitment of resources that the file's own cover note describes, almost apologetically, as "disproportionate to the officially stated cause of death once determined," a sentence that reads, after everything else in this folder, less like an apology and more like an admission that somebody, somewhere above the investigator, already suspected this was not an ordinary accident long before the official language ever caught up to that suspicion.

[BLOK6_KISA_SAPMA_AVALANCHE]
A short digression, because a scientific theory attached to this file, developed by analysts working years after the event with methods not available to the original investigation, deserves the same fair hearing I have given every official and unofficial explanation across this drawer.

A slab avalanche theory, first raised by some of the original searchers themselves and refined considerably in the analysis appended to this file, proposes that an unusual, delayed slab of snow and ice above the campsite could have released with enough force to injure some occupants severely while leaving the tent fabric itself largely intact, panicking the survivors into cutting their way out rather than struggling with a jammed entrance, and fleeing downhill into conditions that killed the rest through ordinary, catastrophic exposure. I want to give this theory real credit, because unlike some of the explanations I have shown you across this drawer, it was developed by people with no apparent motive beyond genuine scientific curiosity, using terrain and snow-load modeling considerably more sophisticated than anything available in nineteen fifty-nine.

What this theory, even in its most carefully modeled form, has not fully reconciled, in my own reading of the file, is the deliberate, controlled nature of the cuts in the tent fabric, the clothing found still inside it, the small fire and apparent climbing activity at the cedar tree well after any avalanche would have passed, and the radiation readings on clothing belonging to the last four recovered. An avalanche is a single, violent, bounded event. This file describes a sequence of deliberate, spaced-out actions — cutting, walking, building a fire, climbing a tree, apparently returning toward the tent — that unfolded over a period of hours, by people who, if injured as severely and as early as the avalanche theory requires for at least some of them, should not have been capable of the sustained, coordinated activity the forensic timeline otherwise describes.

I want to note one further detail about the theory's own authors, because I think it is a fair thing to raise. Even the analysts who wrote the avalanche explanation into this file acknowledge the radiation readings and the cedar tree activity as separate, loosely connected puzzles rather than fully folding them into the avalanche model itself, treating them, in their own appended notes, as unfortunate coincidences layered on top of an otherwise sufficient explanation. I find that kind of selective fit, applied honestly by people with no motive to deceive, almost more unsettling than a deliberate cover-up would be, because it suggests that even a genuinely careful, scientific reading of this file cannot quite make every piece of it lie down in the same bed without leaving something uncomfortably exposed at the edges.

[BLOK7_NE_OLDUKLARI]
One opinion per file, and I want to hold this one carefully, because I think this is a file where my own instinct and the available evidence pull in slightly different directions, and I owe you that tension rather than a false confidence.

I think something drove this group to cut their way out of a secure shelter, in the dark, in deadly cold, deliberately rather than in blind panic, and that whatever it was frightened them enough to accept near-certain death by exposure as preferable to remaining inside. I do not think an avalanche alone explains the controlled cuts or the hours of subsequent activity. I am less certain than I have been on almost any other file in this drawer about what to call the thing that frightened them, and I think, for once, that uncertainty is the most honest thing I can offer you rather than a theory dressed up to sound more settled than it is.

I want to add one more consideration before I move past my own opinion, because I think it is worth naming even though I cannot resolve it. Light, sound, or pressure phenomena associated with certain kinds of weapons testing, were any such testing occurring in that remote a region at that time, could plausibly produce exactly the kind of sudden, disorienting fear that drives people to make exactly the sort of controlled, hurried, non-panicked exit this file describes — a response to something specific and directional rather than a formless terror. I have found no direct evidence of such testing in this file, and I want to be honest that this is speculation rather than a documented finding. I include it only because it is the one explanation I have been able to construct that accounts for both the deliberate cut and the group's shared sense of a direction to move away from, without requiring anything this drawer has not already shown me evidence of in other files.
