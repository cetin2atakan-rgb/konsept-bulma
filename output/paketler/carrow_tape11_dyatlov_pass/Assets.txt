## DESCRIPTION
The Carrow Institute is a work of fiction inspired by real events and decades of unexplained lore. The archive, its records officer, and its people are invented. Real historical persons appear in dramatized form.

The northern Ural Mountains, the night of 1 to 2 February 1959. On the slope of a peak whose name in the local Mansi language means Dead Mountain, nine experienced hikers cut their own tent open from the inside and walk away from it into the snow, most of them in socks, leaving their boots and coats behind. None of them come back. A found-footage mystery audio drama, recorded in a records office basement in 1977.

Thursday, the 25th of August, 1977. Twelve minutes past midnight.

For the first time, Raymond Keel opens a file that did not come from his own country. It arrived through "a reciprocal exchange arrangement with a counterpart office": a translated Soviet investigation with a tent cut from the inside, footprints that walk instead of run, a fire under a cedar tree, radiation on the clothing of the last four found, and an official cause of death that names nothing. In its margin, in the same pencil hand from two earlier tapes, is a note saying Carrow's office asked for this file by name.
