## Description
What does a full year of regular swimming actually do to a body over 50? Month by month: the heart, the joints, the bones and the shoulders, based on the clinical trials.
Swimming lowered blood pressure and eased arthritis pain in these studies, but it did not build bone, and the biggest joint benefit faded once people stopped showing up.

This video follows two versions of the same 65-year-old woman through twelve months. One gets in the pool on an ordinary Tuesday, one goes back to the car. At month one the joint's workload drops and the heart gets a training stimulus it rarely gets on land. At month three resting blood pressure falls in a 12-week trial, while the lab fitness score stays flat. At month six the joint story and the bone story split. At month nine there is the fading trial result and the shoulder caveat. At month twelve: the full accounting, system by system.

Do you swim for the joints, the heart, or just because you like the water? Tell me in the comments.

Studies referenced in this video:
- Nualnim et al. (2012), American Journal of Cardiology: swimming and blood pressure in adults over 50, n=43, 12 weeks
- Sheffield trial: swimming and vascular function, n=40, 8 weeks
- Swimming vs cycling osteoarthritis trial: joint pain and function, n=48, 12 weeks
- UK water-based exercise RCT: hip and knee osteoarthritis, n=312, 1 year and 18 months
- Bone density reviews and masters athlete comparisons (swimmers vs runners)
- Masters swimmers shoulder pain surveys, n=282, 3 years

This is general education, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before starting a swimming program, especially if you have a heart condition, a joint replacement, or take blood pressure medication.
