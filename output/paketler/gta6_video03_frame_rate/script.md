## BEAT 4 — Why the Pro cannot save you — 2:58–4:01

> Now the important part. Why a nine hundred dollar PS five Pro does not fix this.
>
> Most people assume frame rate is a graphics problem. More graphics power, more frames. For a lot of games, that is roughly true.
>
> Open world games are different. What costs you frames in a city is not the lighting. It is the simulation. Every pedestrian deciding where to walk. Every car routing through traffic. Physics. Audio. The script running on every single thing in range.
>
> That work happens on the CPU. And a stronger graphics chip does not help the CPU at all.
>
> Here is the problem with the Pro. Its graphics chip is a serious step up. Its CPU is barely different. Digital Foundry described the processor gain over a base PS five as fractional.
>
> So if GTA six is at thirty because the CPU is saturated simulating Vice City — and in an open world this dense, that is the likely answer — then the Pro gives you a better looking thirty. Not a sixty.
>
> One analyst put it plainly. Sixty, quote, feels like a bridge too far.
>
> That is why I would not buy a Pro for this game. Not because the Pro is bad. Because you would be spending nine hundred dollars on the half of the machine that is not the bottleneck.

## BEAT 6 — Forty — 4:27–5:42

> So where did the community actually land? Not sixty. Forty.
>
> A forty frames mode is the realistic hope, and the technical analysts have been hoping for it too.
>
> Forty is a real jump in feel. At thirty, every frame is on screen for about thirty-three milliseconds. At forty it is twenty-five. You feel that in the steering.
>
> But there is a catch most people do not mention. A forty frames mode needs a one hundred and twenty hertz display. If your TV is sixty hertz, forty frames cannot be presented properly, and the mode does nothing for you.
>
> And there is a second gate on it that almost nobody mentions at all. The scenario the analysts are describing is a hundred and twenty hertz plus variable refresh rate. VRR.
>
> Those are two separate things. A TV can do one without the other, and VRR is a setting you can have switched off on your console without ever noticing it.
>
> So it is two checks, not one. Does the panel do a hundred and twenty hertz, and is VRR actually on.
>
> I am spelling that out because if a forty mode does arrive, the people who get it will be the ones who checked in advance. Not the ones who find out on the nineteenth.
>
> So before you get excited about a forty mode — check your TV, not your console.
>
> And to be clear, Rockstar has not announced a forty mode either. Nobody has confirmed it. It is the thing the people who understand the hardware are hoping for, which is a different thing from news.
