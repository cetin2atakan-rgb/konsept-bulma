1. You train hard, you press heavy, yet in a plain t-shirt your shoulders still look narrow.
2. That is frustrating, because wide shoulders are one of the first things people notice about a man.
3. The good news is that narrow looking shoulders are often a training problem, not a life sentence.
4. Today we cover six mistakes that keep shoulders looking small, and the plain fixes backed by research.
5. Some of these mistakes happen in the gym, and one of them happens every time you stand up.
6. Fix them, and the same frame can look broader, stronger, and more solid from every angle.
7. I am Coach Hal, and this channel is about strength you can see, built with honest training.
8. Stay to the end, because the last part turns all six fixes into one simple weekly plan.
9. First, let us look at what actually makes shoulders look wide when someone sees you.
10. Part of it is bone, the width of your collarbones and shoulder blades, which training cannot change.
11. But the bone frame is only the hanger, the muscle on top decides how the shirt fills.
12. The main shoulder muscle is the deltoid, a rounded cap that wraps around the top of your arm.
13. It has three parts, a front head, a side head, and a rear head, each with a job.
14. The front head lifts your arm forward, and it works hard in every press and push you do.
15. The side head lifts your arm out sideways, and sits at the outer edge of your frame.
16. That outer edge is what people see from the front, so the side head adds visible width.
17. The rear head pulls your arm backward, and gives the shoulder depth when seen from the side.
18. Below the shoulders, the lats are the wide muscles of your back that flare out under your arms.
19. Big lats make your waist look smaller by contrast, which creates the classic tapered upper body shape.
20. And on top of all that sits your posture, which can add or hide inches of visual width.
21. Why does this matter beyond looks, because people read your upper body as a signal of strength.
22. A two thousand seventeen study by Sell and colleagues had people rate photos of men's bodies.
23. Judged physical strength explained over seventy percent of how attractive the men's bodies were rated.
24. In that study, the men who looked strongest were rated most attractive in every sample tested.
25. So building a wider, stronger looking upper body is not vanity, it changes how people read you.
26. Let us go through the mistakes one by one, starting with the most common one in every gym.
27. Mistake one, pressing everything and never training the side of your shoulders directly with its own exercise.
28. Most men think the overhead press and bench press are enough to build big, wide shoulders.
29. Those presses are great lifts, but they mainly load the front head of the deltoid.
30. The side head, the part that creates width, often gets less work than you think from pressing alone.
31. A two thousand twenty study by Coratella and colleagues measured shoulder muscle activity in competitive bodybuilders.
32. The frontal raise produced the highest front deltoid activity of the exercises they tested.
33. Lateral raise variations shifted the work between the shoulder heads, depending on how the arm was turned.
34. The authors concluded that each raise variation targets the shoulder in its own way, so choice matters.
35. A two thousand twenty five study by Larsen and colleagues, including Brad Schoenfeld, tested lateral raises.
36. For eight weeks, trained lifters raised one arm with a dumbbell and the other with a cable.
37. Side deltoid thickness grew in both arms, and the dumbbell and cable versions worked about equally well.
38. So you do not need fancy equipment, a pair of light dumbbells is enough to start.
39. The lateral raise is simple, stand tall, lift your arms out to the sides, stop near shoulder height.
40. Use a weight you can control, because swinging a heavy dumbbell moves the work away from the shoulder.
41. Lead with your elbows, keep a slight bend in your arms, and lower the weight slowly.
42. This one exercise targets the exact part of the shoulder that makes you look wider from the front.
43. Mistake two, ignoring your rear deltoids, the small muscles at the back of your shoulders.
44. Most men train what they see in the mirror, so the back of the shoulder gets forgotten.
45. But the rear head gives the shoulder its round, full look from the side and from behind.
46. A two thousand fifteen study had twelve trained men do rows, pulldowns, and the reverse peck deck.
47. The reverse peck deck produced the highest rear deltoid activity of the three exercises tested.
48. An earlier two thousand thirteen study by Botton and colleagues found the reverse peck deck ahead as well.
49. The reverse peck deck is a machine where you open your arms backward, like spreading wide wings.
50. At home, a bent over reverse fly with light dumbbells trains the same movement pattern.
51. Hinge forward with a flat back, let the dumbbells hang, then raise them out and back.
52. Keep the weight light, because the rear deltoid is a small muscle and loses tension quickly.
53. Training the rear delts and upper back also balances out all the pressing you already do.
54. And here is a rule that applies to every exercise in this video and on this channel.
55. If any shoulder movement causes sharp pain, stop, and talk to a doctor or physical therapist.
56. Shoulders are complex joints, so pain is a signal to get checked, not something to push through.
57. Mistake three, doing too few total sets for your shoulders across the week to make them grow.
58. Many men do two quick sets of raises at the end of a workout and call it done.
59. A two thousand seventeen review by Schoenfeld, Ogborn, and Krieger studied weekly training volume and muscle growth.
60. They found that more weekly sets per muscle were linked to more muscle growth in the studies reviewed.
61. Fewer than five weekly sets were linked to about five point four percent growth on average.
62. Five to nine sets were linked to about six point six percent, a modest step up.
63. Ten or more weekly sets were linked to about nine point eight percent, the largest gains they saw.
64. The review could not test much higher volumes, because few studies had looked at them at the time.
65. It also included few experienced lifters, so treat these numbers as a general guide, not a rule.
66. Still, the message is clear, a couple of sets a week is probably not enough for real change.
67. Count your direct side and rear delt sets across the whole week, not just in one workout.
68. If the number is very low, adding sets gradually is one of the simplest changes you can make.
69. Add them slowly, so your shoulders and elbows have time to get used to the extra work.
70. More is not always better forever, but most men start far below the range linked to growth.
71. Mistake four, training your shoulders only once a week, on one long and exhausting shoulder day.
72. The classic bodybuilding split gives each muscle one big day, then leaves it alone for a week.
73. A two thousand sixteen review by Schoenfeld and colleagues compared training frequencies for muscle growth.
74. Training a muscle group at least twice a week was linked to more growth than once a week.
75. There was not enough data to say whether going even higher than that adds more growth.
76. The authors also noted that in many studies, the higher frequency groups did more total weekly sets.
77. So part of the benefit may simply come from more total work spread across more sessions.
78. That still helps you, because two shorter shoulder sessions make it easier to reach a good weekly volume.
79. Your sets stay fresher too, because you are not cramming everything into one exhausting workout.
80. A simple approach is to add a few raises to two different training days each week.
81. That could be lateral raises on upper body days and reverse flyes on another day.
82. You do not need a separate shoulder day, just spread the same work across the week.
83. Small, frequent doses fit easily into busy schedules, which is exactly what most working men need.
84. Mistake five, forgetting the lats, the big back muscles that shape the outline of your upper body.
85. Shoulders look wide partly by contrast, so what happens below them changes how wide they appear.
86. When the lats are developed, they flare out under your arms and make your waist look narrower.
87. That tapered shape makes the shoulders above look even broader, without adding a single inch of bone.
88. Remember the Sell study, judged strength explained most of how attractive the men's bodies were rated.
89. A wide back is one of the clearest visual cues of upper body strength that people pick up.
90. The lats are trained with pulling movements, where you pull your arms down or back toward your body.
91. Pull ups and chin ups are classic options, and they use only your bodyweight and a bar.
92. Lat pulldowns on a machine let you choose a lighter weight while you build up your strength.
93. Rows, with a cable, a barbell, or a dumbbell, train the lats and the upper back together.
94. If you can, pair every pressing movement with a pulling one, so your back keeps up.
95. A balanced upper body looks wider and more athletic than one built only from pushing exercises.
96. Mistake six, slouching, the mistake that makes even well trained shoulders look narrow every day.
97. When your shoulders round forward and your chest caves in, your upper body takes up less space.
98. You can spend months building muscle, then hide it all by how you stand and sit.
99. A two thousand sixteen study by Vacharkulksemsuk and colleagues looked at posture and first impressions.
100. In speed dating, people with more open, expansive posture were more likely to get a yes.
101. In a dating app test, expansive photos were about twenty seven percent more likely to get a yes.
102. The researchers linked this to two impressions, open posture looked more dominant and more open.
103. These were first impressions only, but first impressions are exactly where shoulder width gets noticed.
104. The fix is simple, stand tall, let your shoulder blades settle back and down, chest lifted gently.
105. Do not force a stiff military pose, just take up your natural space instead of shrinking.
106. Your rear delt and upper back work from mistake two make this position easier to hold.
107. Check your posture when you walk into a room, sit at a desk, or stand in a photo.
108. Of all six fixes, this is the one that changes how you look within seconds.
109. Now let us put all six fixes together into one simple weekly plan you can follow.
110. Here is the plan, and it works with whatever training split you already use.
111. Keep your presses, they build strength, but add direct side delt work with lateral raises.
112. Add rear delt work with reverse flyes or a reverse peck deck, using a light, controlled weight.
113. Spread that shoulder work across at least two training days each week, not one long session.
114. Build your weekly sets gradually, remembering the review that linked ten or more weekly sets to more growth.
115. Pair your pressing with pulling, using pull ups, pulldowns, or rows to build wider lats.
116. And every day, outside the gym, stand tall and take up your natural space.
117. Write down your sets each week, so you know your shoulders are really getting enough work.
118. Give it time, because muscle grows slowly, and steady weeks matter more than one hard session.
119. None of this is a magic trick, it is simply training the parts most men forget.
120. Your bones set the frame, but your training and your posture decide how that frame looks.
