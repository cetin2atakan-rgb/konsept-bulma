2. That is one of the most common frustrations in any gym, and the reason is surprisingly simple.
3. A big part of the size of your upper arm does not come from the biceps at all.
4. It comes from two muscles that most men barely train on purpose, or skip completely.

6. Fix this, and your arms can look thicker from the front, from the side, and from behind.

9. First, let us look at the map, because you cannot build what you do not understand.
10. Your upper arm has muscles on the front that bend the elbow, and muscles behind that straighten it.
11. The biceps sits on the front, and it is the muscle everyone flexes in the mirror.
12. But underneath the biceps lies a flatter, wider muscle that most men never think about, the brachialis.
13. The brachialis attaches lower on your forearm and works every time you bend your elbow.
14. When it grows, it pushes the biceps up and outward, making the whole arm look thicker.
15. From the side, a developed brachialis shows as a ridge between your biceps and triceps.
16. On the back of your arm sits the triceps, a large muscle with three separate heads.
17. The triceps covers most of the back of your upper arm, from the shoulder down to the elbow.
18. One of its heads, the long head, starts above the shoulder joint, at the shoulder blade.
19. That detail matters a lot, because it changes which exercises train the long head best.
20. So if you only train biceps, you are training a fraction of your arm and ignoring the rest.
21. Arms look bigger when the back and sides of the arm are full, not just the peak.
22. And the good news is that both of these muscles respond well to simple, direct training.
23. Think of your arm like a cylinder, it only looks thick when every side is built up.
24. Let us go through the five exercises one by one, starting with the most important for size.
25. Number one, the overhead triceps extension, the move that trains the long head at full stretch.
26. You hold a weight above your head and bend your elbows, lowering it behind your head.
27. Because the long head starts above the shoulder, raising your arm overhead puts it in a long position.
28. A two thousand twenty three study by Maeo and colleagues tested exactly that idea.
29. Twenty one adults trained triceps for twelve weeks, one arm overhead and the other at the side.
30. Both arms did the same cable elbow extension, with the same sets and reps, twice a week.
31. The overhead arm grew about one point four times more triceps size than the arm at the side.
32. The difference was especially clear in the long head, the part that starts above the shoulder.
33. That is a big result from a simple change in arm position, using the same basic movement.
34. You can do this with a cable, a dumbbell held in both hands, or a band at home.
35. Stand or sit tall, brace your stomach, and keep your elbows pointing forward, not flaring wide.
36. Lower the weight slowly behind your head until you feel a deep stretch behind the arm.
37. Then straighten your arms fully, squeezing the triceps at the top without arching your lower back.
38. Start light, because the stretched position is demanding on the elbows and shoulders at first.
39. And here is a rule that applies to every exercise in this video and on this channel.
40. If anything causes sharp pain in your elbow or shoulder, stop, and see a doctor or physical therapist.
41. Elbows can get irritated when training volume jumps too quickly, so build up gradually over weeks.
42. Lowering a little less deeply at first is fine while your elbows adapt to the stretch.
43. Warm up with a light set first, because cold elbows do not enjoy heavy stretched positions.
44. Number two, the close grip press, a heavy compound move that loads your triceps hard.
45. A normal bench press works the chest, shoulders, and triceps, but grip width changes the balance.
46. A two thousand five study by Lehman measured muscle activity during bench press variations in twelve men.
47. Moving from a wide grip to a narrower grip increased triceps activity in his study.
48. The narrow grip also decreased activity in part of the chest, shifting more work onto the triceps.
49. That makes a closer grip a simple way to turn a chest exercise into a triceps builder.
50. Close does not mean hands touching, about shoulder width is enough for most men.
51. Keep your elbows tucked closer to your body as you lower the bar to your lower chest.
52. At home, a close hand push up does the same job, with hands under your shoulders.
53. Press up smoothly and lock out your arms at the top, feeling the triceps finish the move.
54. Because you can use heavier loads here, this exercise builds strength as well as size.
55. Pair it with your chest training, and your triceps will get heavy work every single week.
56. If your wrists complain, turn your hands slightly, or use dumbbells with your palms facing each other.
57. Number three, the triceps pushdown, the simple cable or band move that finishes the triceps off.
58. In the Maeo study, the arm trained at the side still grew, just less than the overhead arm.
59. So pushdowns are not useless, they are a good second triceps exercise after overhead work.
60. Stand tall facing a cable or a band anchored above you, elbows tight to your sides.
61. Push the handle down until your arms are straight, then let it rise slowly under control.
62. Keep your elbows still, because letting them swing turns the move into a shoulder exercise.
63. Pushdowns are easy on the joints for most men, so they work well at the end of sessions.
64. Combining one overhead move with one pushdown or press covers all three triceps heads well.
65. Bands are cheap and easy to anchor on a door, so this move works at home as well.
66. Number four, the hammer curl, the curl that brings the brachialis into the spotlight.
67. A hammer curl is a dumbbell curl with your palms facing each other, like holding a hammer.
68. Because the brachialis attaches to the forearm bone that does not rotate, it works in every grip.
69. With palms facing up, the biceps takes much of the load and gets most of the attention.
70. With palms facing each other, many coaches find the brachialis and forearm take more of the work.
71. The research here is limited, so think of hammer curls as smart variety rather than a proven secret.
72. Still, the brachialis only grows if you load it, and hammer curls are a simple way.
73. Stand tall, keep your elbows by your sides, and curl the dumbbells up without swinging your body.
74. Lower them slowly, because the lowering part is where many men cut corners and lose tension.
75. Reverse curls, with palms facing down, also load the brachialis and the top of your forearm.
76. Use a lighter weight for reverse curls, because the grip makes them much harder than normal curls.
77. Try alternating hammer curls and normal curls across the week, so every elbow flexor gets work.
78. Number five, chin ups and pulldowns, the big pulling moves that build your arms without a single curl.
79. Every time you pull your body up or pull a bar down, your elbows bend under heavy load.
80. A two thousand fifteen study by Gentil and colleagues compared lat pulldowns with biceps curls in untrained men.
81. After ten weeks, the pulldown group and the curl group gained similar elbow flexor muscle thickness.
82. In that study, a compound pulling move built the front of the arm about as well as curls.
83. A related study by de Franca and colleagues found similar results in trained men over eight weeks.
84. So heavy pulling is a powerful arm builder, and it trains your back at the same time.
85. Chin ups, with palms facing you, put the elbow flexors in a strong position to work hard.
86. Pull your chest toward the bar, then lower yourself all the way down under control.
87. If chin ups are too hard, use a band for help, or start with lat pulldowns instead.
88. Pulling and pressing build the base, and direct arm work like overhead extensions adds the finishing detail.
89. Heavy pull ups and rows, done for years, can build thick arms and forearms with few curls.
90. Now, let us talk about the most common arm training mistakes, so you avoid them from the start.
91. The first is doing endless biceps curls while giving the triceps one rushed set at the end.
92. Remember, the triceps covers most of the back of the arm, so it deserves at least equal attention.
93. The second is swinging heavy weights with your whole body, which takes the work away from the arms.
94. If your back moves more than your elbows, the weight is too heavy for good arm training.
95. The third is cutting the range short, especially missing the deep stretch on overhead extensions.
96. The Maeo result suggests the stretched position matters, so use a full, controlled range.
97. The fourth is training arms only once a week, in one long session that leaves them exhausted.
98. Remember from our shoulder video, training a muscle at least twice a week was linked to more growth.
99. Spreading the same work over two sessions also keeps each set fresher and higher in quality.
100. And finally, many men forget that arms grow from progress, not from pump alone.
101. Add a rep or a little weight over time, and keep your effort honest on every working set.
102. Small, steady progress on arm exercises adds up faster than most men expect over a few months.
103. Now let us put all five exercises together into one simple weekly plan you can follow.
104. Here is the plan, and it fits into whatever training split you already use.
105. Train your arms directly on at least two days each week, rather than one long arm day.
106. Start your triceps work with an overhead extension, using a full and controlled stretch.
107. Add close grip presses or close hand push ups on at least one day for heavy triceps work.
108. Finish with pushdowns when you have time, keeping your elbows still and the movement smooth.
109. For the front of the arm, include chin ups or pulldowns in your back training every week.
110. Add hammer curls or reverse curls to give the brachialis and forearms direct work.
111. Keep your form strict, your range full, and add a rep or a little weight over time.
112. Write down your arm exercises each session, so you can see your progress clearly.
113. Give it a few months, because arms grow steadily, not overnight, even with perfect training.
114. Take a photo of your arms from the side today, so you can compare honestly in three months.
115. None of this is a magic trick, it is simply training the parts of the arm men forget.
116. Your biceps will still be there, but now the whole arm will finally match it.
117. And bigger arms look best when they are also strong, so keep chasing real strength too.
