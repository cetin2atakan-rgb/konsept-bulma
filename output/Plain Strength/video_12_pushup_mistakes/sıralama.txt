10. First, can push ups really build muscle like a bench press, or is that just a myth.
11. A two thousand seventeen study by Kikuchi and Nakazato compared push ups with a lighter bench press.
12. Over eight weeks, both groups gained similar strength and similar chest and triceps muscle thickness.
13. A two thousand fifteen study by Calatayud and colleagues used push ups with elastic bands for extra resistance.
14. When muscle activity was matched to a heavy bench press, both groups gained similar bench press strength.
15. So yes, push ups can build real muscle and strength, but only under one important condition.
16. That condition is simple to understand, but most men never actually apply it to their training.
17. They have to be hard enough, and that is exactly where most men go wrong.
18. Your chest, shoulders, and triceps do not know the difference between a barbell and the floor.
19. They only respond to how much tension they feel and how close to failure each set gets.
20. That is good news, because tension and effort are things you can control on any floor.

22. Let us go through the six mistakes one by one, starting with the most common of all.
23. Mistake one, doing easy push ups far from failure, and stopping long before your muscles are really tired.
24. Many men knock out twenty easy push ups, stop while they could do ten more, and move on.
25. Remember from our effort video, sets that stop far from failure likely leave muscle growth behind.
26. A two thousand seventeen review by Schoenfeld and colleagues found light loads build muscle when pushed hard.
27. Push ups are a light load for many men, so they work best when taken close to failure.
28. If your last few push ups are not slow and hard, your set was probably too easy.
29. The fix is to push each working set until only a rep or two of good form remains.
30. If that takes fifty push ups, the exercise is too easy, and you need a harder version.
31. Being able to do fifty push ups is impressive, but it is not the best way to grow.
32. Mistake two, never making your push ups harder, and doing the same version year after year.
33. Your body adapts quickly, so the push up that was hard last year may be easy now.
34. Doing the same easy version forever is like lifting the same light dumbbell for years.
35. A two thousand eleven study by Ebben and colleagues measured the force different push up variations demand.
36. They measured force through the hands for standard push ups, knee push ups, and elevated versions.
37. Push ups with the feet raised on a box produced the highest force of all the variations.
38. Push ups with the hands raised, or from the knees, produced the lowest force.
39. So simply changing the height of your feet or hands changes how heavy the push up feels.
40. That gives you a ladder, from hands on a bench, to the floor, to feet on a box.
41. You can also add a resistance band across your back, just like in the Calatayud study.
42. Or wear a backpack with books or water bottles inside to add weight a little at a time.
43. A weighted vest is another option, letting you add load in small steps over many weeks.
44. A two thousand eighteen study by Kotarsky and colleagues used progressive push up variations for four weeks.
45. The push up group got clearly stronger, both on push ups and on the bench press.
46. Four weeks was not enough to show a significant change in muscle thickness, so be patient.
47. The lesson is simple, keep climbing the push up ladder, and give your muscles time to grow.
48. And here is a rule that applies to every exercise in this video and on this channel.
49. If anything causes sharp pain in your shoulders, elbows, or wrists, stop and see a doctor.
50. Add difficulty gradually, especially with bands and weighted backpacks, so your joints can adapt.
51. Wrist pain is common at first, and push up handles or holding dumbbells can keep wrists straight.
52. Mistake three, placing your hands in a random spot, often far too wide with elbows flaring out.
53. Many men believe a very wide grip builds a bigger chest, but research does not clearly support it.
54. A two thousand five study by Cogley and colleagues compared narrow, normal, and wide hand positions.
55. In their study, the narrow hand position produced higher chest and triceps activity than the wide one.
56. Other studies have found mixed results, so hand width is not a magic switch for the chest.
57. One study found narrow hands mainly raised triceps work, while another favored wide hands for the chest.
58. But very wide hands with elbows flared straight out often feel uncomfortable on the shoulders.
59. A safer starting point is hands slightly wider than your shoulders, with elbows angled back a little.
60. Think of your body forming an arrow from above, not a letter T with your arms.
61. Narrower push ups shift more work onto the triceps, so use them when you want arm work too.
62. Try a few hand positions, and use the one that feels strongest and most comfortable for your shoulders.
63. Mistake four, cutting the range of motion short, and doing tiny half reps near the top.
64. Many men lower themselves only halfway down, which makes every push up much easier than it should be.
65. Your chest gets its biggest stretch near the bottom, and skipping it wastes much of the movement.
66. Half reps also make it easy to fool yourself about how strong you really are.
67. Remember from our arms video, training a muscle in its stretched position looked important for growth.
68. Lower yourself until your chest is close to the floor, then press back up to straight arms.
69. If you cannot reach the bottom with good form, use a harder full range version later.
70. Start with hands on a bench or a step, and work through the full range there first.
71. A slow lowering, around two or three seconds, also makes each rep harder and more controlled.
72. Full range with a slower tempo can turn even easy push ups into a serious challenge.
73. Pause briefly at the bottom without resting on the floor, then press up with control.
74. Mistake five, letting your hips sag or pike up, so your body bends like a banana.
75. A push up is also a moving plank, so your core keeps your body in a straight line.
76. When your hips sag, your lower back takes stress, and your chest and arms do less work.
77. When your hips pike up, you shorten the movement and turn it into an easier variation.
78. Squeeze your glutes and brace your stomach, as if someone is about to push on your belly.
79. Breathe in on the way down, and breathe out as you press back up to the top.
80. Keep your head in line with your spine, looking at the floor slightly ahead of your hands.
81. Your body should move as one solid unit, from your heels to the back of your head.
82. If you cannot hold that position, drop to a hands raised version until your core catches up.
83. A few weeks of planks and hands raised push ups usually build the core strength you need.
84. Mistake six, doing only push ups, and never training the pulling muscles on the other side.
85. Push ups build the chest, shoulders, and triceps, but the back works the opposite way.
86. If you only push, your upper body training ends up lopsided, with your back left behind.
87. Remember from our narrow shoulders video, a strong upper back helps you stand tall and look wider.
88. Pair your push ups with rows, using a sturdy table, a band, or a pull up bar.
89. For every set of push ups, try to do a set of rows or pulls as well.
90. That balance keeps your training even, and your upper body looking strong from every angle.
91. Rows also help your push ups, because a strong back gives your shoulders a stable base.
